# Creative concepts

Find something worth saying or showing about this product in someone's life, then invent its expression. A benefit is the commercial direction. A format such as packing, POV, unboxing or a skit is the container. The creative premise is the particular observation, question, pleasure or argument developed inside it.

## Start with the brief and usable material

Carry the user's audience, market, language, purpose, duration and explicit requirements. Separate user choices from earlier agents' suggestions. Read original product material and supplied research. Mstudio has no network search capability; work from imported evidence. For TikTok or shoppable short video, read the TikTok section in [source-to-idea notes](references/diagnosis.md); use the other cases when their creative question is relevant.

Notice how someone actually chooses, combines, handles, enjoys or makes room for the product. Preserve particular preferences, routines, buying questions and workarounds from the material. “Busy people need convenience” has discarded most of the useful information. When audience evidence is sparse, a modest fictional use situation can supply creative material; do not present it as a discovered customer insight.

Keep confirmed properties, reported experience and invention distinct. A photo can establish a visible structure, not a duration or comparative performance result. A fictional demonstration or time jump cannot establish either. Ask only for unknowns that materially change what can be written.

## Choose what this advertisement will make interesting

Connect a specific audience choice or desire to a supported product contribution. Work at the scale of the product: what can someone do, bring, arrange, notice, feel or express with it? Separate what the product enables from what the person, another accessory or the surrounding situation causes. This supplies the commercial meaning before inventing a hook.

Choose an angle with something to discover even when the category is familiar. Useful material includes an unresolved buying question, a revealing way of using it, an overlooked detail with a consequence, or a pleasurable experience worth repeating. A desire or point of view can be enough; do not manufacture a hardship to justify a useful object. Brand-platform briefs can draw meaning from supplied positioning and people's relationship to the product without a functional demonstration.

Explore a few genuinely different premises in rough form: the exact observation or opening action, the substance that follows, and what it leaves the viewer understanding or wanting. Change the viewing experience or argument, not merely the location of the same feature tour. Choose from the material before composing a timed script. This exploration need not be shown unless alternatives were requested.

## Turn the angle into content

Give the viewer an immediate reason to join the experience and then deliver on it. A practical question earns attention through a useful answer; a process through its particular transformations and result; a viewpoint through recognisable implications; humour through an observation or behaviour that develops. These are possible ways of writing, not a mandatory sequence.

For creator-led short video, decide what this person cares about enough to show someone else. That priority determines the objects, words and omissions. Let product use carry the evidence while the speaker supplies context, preference or interpretation. A face, slang or a “POV” label alone supplies no perspective. Fictional creator-style copy must not imply real purchase history, measured results or customer testimony.

Develop the viewing promise and commercial meaning together. If the interest is a particular process, write the consequential steps or variations; if it is a buying distinction, show the comparison that clarifies it; if it is enjoyment, develop the actual sensations. Do not add unrelated spectacle to get attention and then restart as a generic sales pitch. Ordinary use can be sufficient material when its specificity, information or expression rewards watching.

Compose decisive words, actions and sound relationships now. Let an action, sentence or pattern continue across the piece. An answer can arrive early; withholding, conflict, escalation, a second character and a reversal are creative options. Select details that develop this idea, rather than spending the duration covering every feature. End by completing the experience or offering its natural next step.

## Deliver and hand off

Briefly state the communication focus and product basis, then preserve the actual opening, development and ending in the handoff, including decisive wording, objects, behaviour and meaningful sound. Separate requirements from adjustable choices. A slogan or abstract synopsis alone loses the creative material.

Continue to screenwriting when the user requested a complete script. Deliver the work without a separate concept approval, scoring exercise or self-critique stage. Concepts remain conversation content unless a supported save is requested. This Skill does not author timed screenplay or shot records, model prompts, media or timelines, and grants no additional permissions.
