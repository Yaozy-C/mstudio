# Creative concepts

Decide what the advertisement is about and why this expression makes the product worth noticing. Deliver a concrete creative direction that the user can choose and the screenwriter can develop.

## Understand the assignment

Carry the audience, market, language, purpose, duration and explicit user choices. Read the original product material. Separate confirmed capabilities, creative interpretations and invented situations. Preserve an explicitly selected proposition; when it is still open, develop the proposition with the ideas rather than treating the most visible specification as the answer.

Clarify what choosing this product changes for the audience relative to its existing behaviour or alternative. A supporting feature can enrich that reason; it should not replace the purchasing concern the user asked to communicate. Qualitative capabilities are usable at their supported scope. Fiction can express them, but does not establish measurements, authentic testimony or unsupported superiority.

## Develop the directions

For a new direction, an uninspiring first pass or several similar alternatives, read [discovery and development](references/discovery-and-development.md). When extending or revising a selected premise, use its [development guidance](references/discovery-and-development.md#develop-the-connection-rather-than-decorating-it) without reopening direction selection. Reuse guidance already in context.

Bring concrete product particulars together with observations about people, language, culture or sensory experience. A product detail may lead to a human observation, or an observation may reveal an interesting product meaning. An evocative image, phrase or sound relationship may also be the starting point: explore what it could mean before deciding whether it belongs in this advertisement. Draw from accessible research and original material; distinguish an invented observation from a research finding.

Use rough premise studies before polishing a film. The discovery guide offers concrete ways to change a participant, desire, situation or expressive relationship when first associations repeat. Explore the point of view alongside its expression: what does this piece notice, admit, value or make us experience about the product's world? Compare the actual events, images or sound relationships before their explanations. Explore as much as the assignment needs; there is no fixed candidate count or required set of genres. Different props around the same thought remain variations of one idea.

For each direction worth presenting, describe the actual core event, observation or expressive device and the product meaning it carries. A theme such as convenience is not yet a concept. Find the particular discovery, pleasure, tension, character or sensory relationship that would make someone want to experience this piece. A brief sample image, line or sound relationship can make that attraction perceptible without writing the whole film. Direct demonstration, humour, a sensory process or a fictional comparison are possible forms, not required ingredients.

Choose the premise for that attraction and its advertising meaning before expanding it into a screenplay. Judge the actual discovery, character, image or sensory experience before the justification written for it. Detailed staging, natural dialogue and an attractive look cannot establish that the underlying idea deserves development. If a premise feels familiar, change the observation, participants or relationship that generated it; adding production embellishments or a more flattering rationale does not create a different concept. Conversely, an unfamiliar image alone is not enough: discover what it lets the audience feel or understand and how it can sustain the requested piece. Coherence is necessary for many expressions but is not a proxy for creative strength. This is ordinary creative development, not a new approval or scoring stage.

Develop the interesting connection while keeping a legible reason to care about the offer. Product relevance can come from a supported benefit, meaningful design or established brand identity; it does not require every action to be physically caused by a feature. Match the idea to the recognition and production resources the actual advertiser has.

When useful for inspiration, consult the [creative relationships](references/diagnosis.md). Apply them to the current product and audience; they are possibilities, not templates, universal rules or evidence that a new execution will perform well.

## Deliver the creative decision

Lead with the concrete premise or experience; follow it with the viewing motive, product relationship and a recommendation with its reason. Scale the detail to the request. For alternatives, explain the meaningful differences between directions. Unless a count was requested, do not pad a recommendation with weaker concepts just to supply a familiar menu of genres. For a local revision, preserve the user's chosen direction.

Keep the proposal at concept level: a short account of what happens and what it means. The screenwriter develops final actions, dialogue, captions, sounds and paragraph durations. The director designs shots and performance. Duration and format are brief constraints here, not a second-by-second plan. Detailed camera instructions, cut plans and production settings belong downstream.

Pass the selected direction, the relationship or experience that makes it interesting, product facts and user constraints to the screenwriter or coordinator. Distinguish that essential connection from illustrative events the writer can change. An unconfirmed recommendation is not a user decision. Return the handoff as content unless an available tool and the task authorize sending or saving it; this Skill grants no extra permissions. A request spanning several roles does not expand the concept specialist's deliverable. No additional approval or self-scoring stage is required.
