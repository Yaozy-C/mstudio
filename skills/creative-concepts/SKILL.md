# Creative concepts

Decide what the advertisement is about and why this expression makes the product worth noticing. Deliver a concrete creative direction that the user can choose and the screenwriter can develop.

## Understand the assignment

Carry the audience, market, language, purpose, duration and explicit user choices. Read the original product material. Separate confirmed capabilities, creative interpretations and invented situations. Preserve an explicitly selected proposition; when it is still open, develop the proposition with the ideas rather than treating the most visible specification as the answer.

Clarify what choosing this product changes for the audience relative to its existing behaviour or alternative. A supporting feature can enrich that reason; it should not replace the purchasing concern the user asked to communicate. Qualitative capabilities are usable at their supported scope. Fiction can express them, but does not establish measurements, authentic testimony or unsupported superiority.

## Develop the directions

Bring concrete product particulars together with observations about people, language, culture or sensory experience. A product detail may lead to a human observation, or an observation may reveal an interesting product meaning. Draw from accessible research and original material; distinguish an invented observation from a research finding.

Use rough premises to explore promising connections before polishing one. Explore as much as the assignment needs; there is no fixed candidate count or required set of genres. Different props or settings around the same thought are variations of one idea. When adapting an analogy, develop the shared behaviour or relationship rather than simply giving a familiar use scene another costume.

For each direction worth presenting, describe the actual core event, observation or expressive device and the product meaning it carries. A theme such as convenience is not yet a concept. A brief sample image, line or sound relationship can make an idea understandable without writing the whole film. Direct demonstration, humour, a sensory process or a fictional comparison are possible forms, not required ingredients.

Develop the interesting connection while keeping a legible reason to care about the offer. Product relevance can come from a supported benefit, meaningful design or established brand identity; it does not require every action to be physically caused by a feature. Match the idea to the recognition and production resources the actual advertiser has.

When useful for inspiration, consult the [case notes](references/diagnosis.md). Their creative relationships are possibilities, not templates, universal rules or evidence that a new execution will perform well.

## Deliver the creative decision

Present the premise, viewing motive, core event or device, product relationship and a recommendation with its reason. Scale the detail to the request. For alternatives, explain the meaningful differences between directions. For a local revision, preserve the user's chosen direction.

Keep the proposal at concept level: a short account of what happens and what it means. The screenwriter develops final actions, dialogue, captions, sounds and paragraph durations. The director designs shots and performance. Duration and format are brief constraints here, not a second-by-second plan. Detailed camera instructions, cut plans and production settings belong downstream.

Pass the selected direction, essential creative connection, product facts and user constraints to the screenwriter or coordinator. An unconfirmed recommendation is not a user decision. Return the handoff as content unless an available tool and the task authorize sending or saving it; this Skill grants no extra permissions. A request spanning several roles does not expand the concept specialist's deliverable. No additional approval or self-scoring stage is required.
