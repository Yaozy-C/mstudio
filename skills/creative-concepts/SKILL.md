# Creative concepts

## The unit is a scene

A concept is **one playable scene**, not a card, a world, a theme or a montage. The card is only the scene's summary.

If you cannot picture one scene — one place, one continuous event, one protagonist — you do not have a concept yet. A citywide rule, a hero reveal, a photo that started a tradition or "a day in the life of the city" is a **world**, not a scene. Worlds need setup; a 15–30s ad does not have it.

The scene contract:

> **who** / **where** / **want** / **obstacle** / **product action** / **turn** / **cost** / **last line** / **first image** / **last image**

Everything else is optional. If any of these is missing, the scene is not ready.

## The product is the mechanism

The product must **do** something in the scene. Its action — open, share, fit, carry, keep, divide, hand over — must cause the turn. The turn must fail without that action.

- Bear: opens the 25L, reveals a meal for a table, shares it; the accusation turns into an invitation.
- Not enough: the bag is in a photo, on a ledge, on a curb, or mentioned in voice-over. That is a prop, not a mechanism.

Run the **cover test**: cover the product in the storyboard. Does the scene still play? If yes, the product is decoration. Kill or rewrite.

The product's particulars should be necessary: 25L capacity, the front-pocket utensil organiser, the double zipper, the soft body, the way it opens. If any cooler would do, it is a category ad, not this product's ad.

## Truth and brand idea are layers, not substitutes

A scene may carry a **truth** (a specific, slightly uncomfortable human observation) and a **brand idea** (a worldview, not a feature). They must be **enacted by the product action**, not stated in a card or a voice-over.

- Bear truth: scarcity makes people call hospitality theft.
- Bear brand idea: there was never a theft problem; there was a portions problem.
- The truth is visible because the bear shares food; the brand idea is visible because the portions are enough for everyone.

If the truth is a platitude ("everyone eats lunch", "life is busy") or the brand idea is a category line ("for every kind of day"), it is not a layer yet. It is a premise.

## Generate one-scene pitches

For an open brief, write **8–12 one-scene pitches** before developing three. Each pitch is one sentence plus its scene contract. Not a world, not a theme, not a format.

Generate by changing one scene element at a time:

- **Who** — the least likely person, a specific profession, a person with a private want.
- **Where / procedure** — one room, one table, one counter, one vehicle; a procedure with rules.
- **Want / obstacle** — what they want now, what stops them, what it costs.
- **Product action** — what the product does that changes the situation.
- **Turn** — the moment the meaning changes.
- **Cost** — who loses or gives something up.

Do not generate "one idea per format" (ASMR, POV, pack-with-me, three-ways). Do not generate "one idea per world" (a citywide truce, a superhero universe, a future society). A format or a world can be the **expression** of a scene, never the scene itself.

If the pitches converge on the same want, procedure or product action, change the generating element — not the costume.

## Develop three scenes

Develop only the three strongest into full scene cards. Each must be playable in the requested duration, in one location or one hard cut, with one protagonist.

Map the scene to the card:

- `oneLine` — the whole scene in one sentence, with the turn visible.
- `participant` — who; one visible person or a single group with one want. Not "the city" or "everyone".
- `want` — what they want now **and** what stops them.
- `ritual` — where and what procedure; one place, not a world rule.
- `evidence` — the product action and how it causes the turn. A visible action, not a claim.
- `reframe` — the last line or action that changes the meaning of the first.
- `risk` — the dramatic cost and the main production risk.
- `cost` — low / medium / high, with the reason.
- `truth` / `brandIdea` — the layers, only if the product action enacts them.

If a card needs a city, a crowd, a photo, a hero, a before/after montage or a world-building voice-over to make sense, it is not a scene. Kill it before script.

## Kill criteria

Kill a direction when any of these is true:

- **World, not scene**: it needs a citywide rule, a montage, a hero universe, a photo that started a tradition, or more than one location to explain itself.
- **No protagonist**: the participant is "the city", "everyone", "no face, only hands", or a group without one want. (A deliberate visual essay is a separate brief; it still needs a visual argument and a turn.)
- **No obstacle**: the protagonist wants something and immediately gets it; the city stops willingly; no one resists.
- **No product action**: the product is shown, mentioned, photographed or placed, but not used to cause the turn.
- **Cover test fails**: cover the product; the scene still plays.
- **No cost**: no one loses or gives anything up; the scene is a mood, not a story.
- **No turn**: the last line explains the first instead of changing it; a title card is not a turn.
- **Truth/brand idea not enacted**: the card states them, the scene does not show them.
- **Runtime doesn't fit**: the scene needs more setup than the requested duration allows.
- **Format-only**: the pitch is "an ASMR/POV/pack-with-me/three-ways/before-after video" without a protagonist, obstacle and turn.

A competent but unremarkable scene should be named as such. The user can still choose it; they should choose it knowing what it is.

## Worked example: the bear hearing

**Scene contract**

- **Who**: a real adult brown bear, deadpan, never speaking, never cute.
- **Where**: a small-town wildlife committee hearing; one room, one table.
- **Want**: to be understood as a provider, not a thief.
- **Obstacle**: the committee's accusation; 37 sandwiches; it is on trial.
- **Product action**: it opens the 25L, reveals a full meal for a table, and shares it.
- **Turn**: the accusation becomes an invitation; "the problem was the portions."
- **Cost**: its dignity and innocence are on the line; it risks being judged.
- **Last line**: "The problem was the portions."
- **First image**: the bear at the table, the committee's backs in the foreground, the projector screen with a crossed-out sandwich.
- **Last image**: the bear and the committee eating at the picnic table; the bag open.

**Why it works**: the product action causes the turn; the truth (scarcity makes hospitality look like theft) and the brand idea (enough for everyone) are enacted by the shared meal; the last line changes the meaning of the first. It is one scene, one protagonist, one cost.

**What would kill it**: a bear that smiles; a bag that could be swapped for any cooler; a hearing that is only a backdrop; a final line that explains "25L is big" instead of reframing the accusation; a version that needs a citywide montage to explain the truce.

## Negative gallery

These are failures, not references to copy.

**THE SOUND** — ASMR, hands and zippers.
- No protagonist, no obstacle, no cost; the product is a sensory demo.
- It is a format. The scene contract is empty.

**THE TABLE** — packing everything on a table, counting what fits.
- No protagonist, no obstacle, no turn; the product proves capacity but nothing changes for a person.
- It is a demo. The card can be filled, the scene cannot be played.

**THREE WAYS** — the same bag in three lives, cut on the same action.
- No single protagonist, no obstacle, no cost; it is a montage of types.
- It is a casting/format idea, not a scene.

**THE 12 O'CLOCK TRUCE** — a city stops at noon because a superhero eats lunch.
- No single protagonist (the city / a hero who appears at the end).
- No obstacle (the city stops willingly); no cost (no one loses anything).
- No product action (the bag is a photo/prop; no one eats from it).
- The turn is a world rule and a title card, not a character action.
- It is a world, not a scene. A 22s ad cannot establish it.

## When the user rejects a direction

Do not pivot to a new format or a bigger world. Identify which scene element failed:

- **Who** — no one to follow.
- **Want/obstacle** — no active want or no resistance.
- **Product action** — the product did not cause the turn.
- **Turn** — the ending explained instead of changed.
- **Cost** — no one lost anything.
- **Runtime** — the scene needed more setup than the format allows.

Restate the failed element in one line, then change **one** element. If the whole set was rejected, check whether the three scenes were actually different: three worlds, three formats or three versions of the same scene means the set failed.

## Missing information is not a missing scene

Missing product specifications affect **execution**, not whether the scene is worth making.

- Use a stand-in object or a visible prop for the scene; mark what is unconfirmed.
- Do not invent measurements, certifications, testimonials or superior performance.
- Do not retreat to a pure product demo or a world because the specs are missing.
- If the project has product images, read them. Use the actual dimensions, storage layout, material and opening mechanism as the product action; do not claim facts that are not visible.

## Recommend and hand off

End with **one** recommendation: the scene, why it plays, the product action, the turn, the cost and the main risk. State what would change your mind. Ask the user to select, kill or revise; do not advance to screenplay or shots on your own.

When the user selects a scene, pass the scene contract and the card to the screenwriter or coordinator. The writer owns the scene's exact words, action and timing; the director owns the shots. Neither may replace the scene with a world or a montage; if they cannot realize it, they return a conflict.

## Anti-patterns

- A world, a montage, a "day in the life", a citywide rule, a hero universe.
- A format (ASMR, POV, pack-with-me, three-ways, before/after) presented as a concept.
- A collective ("the city", "everyone") used as the protagonist.
- A product that is placed, photographed or mentioned but never used.
- A scene where no one loses or gives anything up.
- A last line that explains the film instead of changing it.
- A card that needs a paragraph of rationale to become interesting.
- A product demo or world used because the product facts are missing.
- An invented insight, trend, testimonial or market claim.
