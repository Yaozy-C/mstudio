# Viewing structure and product value

Determine the purpose first. Ads need a reason to consider the product; a visual opening needs a product relationship and payoff; teaching or natural sharing does not require an invented pain point, promotion or twist.

## Connect value and viewing

Before generating a new concept, identify the audience's task, current behavior, intended change in understanding, supported main claim and its visible demonstration. Product presence alone does not establish benefit. Separate facts from hypotheses and planned verification. If core evidence is missing, obtain it or select a supported claim.

Ask whether removing the emphasized feature would leave the same visible benefit. If so, the demonstration may not show that feature's contribution. This is a relevance check, not a requirement for an exclusive feature.

## Motive and continuity

For a key action, identify the participant's current task, why this action happens now, and where the person/object goes next. Earlier context can supply the motive. Results should advance the task, not require unrelated plot additions to explain arbitrary handling. Surreal openings still need readable internal causality.

An established sequence need not demonstrate value anew in every shot: reactions, anticipation, pauses and spatial setup can support the same main claim. If an operation has no purpose, revise its staging or remove it rather than inventing behavior solely to display a component.

## Organize the film

The shot plan can also carry sequence structure: time, scene purpose, visible event, supporting claim/fact and shot ID. Allocate time to action, reading and meaningful reaction. Follow locked totals; maxima need not be filled and provisional estimates remain adjustable. For detailed pacing use [rhythm](../../creative-ad-director/references/rhythm.md).

Test a questionable shot by asking what viewers lose if it disappears: state change, progression, evidence, recognition or emotion. Merely enriching shot sizes is not sufficient. Merge redundant coverage, preserve purposeful repetition and give outcomes time to read. Do not fill time with unrelated features or repeat the same demonstration.

Place persuasive evidence as early as context allows, without a fixed second or universal story formula. The ending can offer an evidenced next step or simply conclude an instructional/sharing task. Prices, discounts, stock and links need factual support. Text and sound can support meaning, not replace missing visual proof. Adapt language, aspect ratio and interface occlusion to the actual destination.

## Effect evidence

Design reasoning supports a choice, not a claim of increased retention or conversion. If comparing openings later, keep body, audience, duration and distribution conditions comparable and record metric definitions and sample size. Lack of audience data does not block authorized design work.
