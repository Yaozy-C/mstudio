# Develop and test an opening

Use only for a new or redesigned opening. [Creative criteria](creative-bar.md) govern acceptance; categories below are possibilities, not automatic passes. Continuation and local repairs do not reopen ideation.

## Find visible material

Start with real product contours, boundaries, material behavior, actions, scale and spatial relationships. Connect them to the current audience task and intended feeling. If an actually viewed reference or accepted sample exists, test whether its conditions transfer. Reuse relationships and evidence, not the previous product's story or parameters. Identify whether evidence is user preference, frame inspection or audience data. Do not delay the task to build a reference library.

| Possible mechanism | Opening cue and expectation | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Imminent completion | Motion, imbalance or near-contact promises an outcome | Entering before the interesting state or after the change |
| Unusual relationship | Familiar objects form a readable anomaly | Incomprehensible disorder or irrelevant product |
| Reinterpretation | A local detail, scale or occlusion supports an initial judgment | Noise or reflection that never supports a judgment |
| Sensory completion | Material, alignment or patterned motion develops visibly | Filters or an adjective replace actual change |
| Outcome or demonstration | A relevant result or operating difference invites explanation | Unsupported benefit or a mismatch with the requested ambition |

## Compare a few substantive candidates

Use short notes: opening/change/payoff, camera contribution, viewer hypothesis and cue, product role, staging conditions, and failure condition/minimum check. Distinguish the real operation from where the film enters it; action can already be underway if viewers can understand what to anticipate.

Eliminate unsupported or explicitly rejected directions before comparison. Compare concrete viewing value, payoff, product connection and locally testable risks; do not use weighted self-scores as audience evidence. Two or three candidates often suffice. Different angles of the same unchanged idea are not substantive alternatives.

If none works, change a causal or viewing relationship rather than repeatedly changing themes. After two rounds blocked by the same unknown fact or capability, resolve that bottleneck or change route instead of asking the user to supply creativity.

## Test the vulnerable relationship

Identify the question and failure condition before making images. Text-only work checks conditions; a single sample targets the most important relationship; multiple frames cover only necessary state changes. Use supplied motion evidence where available. Previs/video tests require [generation authorization](../SKILL.md#production-handoff); testing does not authorize paid calls or purchased material by itself.

Record what was actually seen or missing, not what the prompt requested. A visual sample strengthens design evidence without proving audience response.

## Repair at the right level

- No initial judgment or meaningful payoff: revise information or event design.
- Readable event but weak emphasis, occlusion or cut: revise composition, timing or path.
- Penetration or structural drift: repair execution while retaining the valid idea.
- Readable stills but unknown speed: retain the motion-validation gap, not endless image retries.

Use [reference transfer](shot-reference-and-transfer.md) when adapting actual footage and [preview inspection](preview-images.md) for image failures. Existing screening records may supply source/time range, actual observation, mechanism and transfer conditions; map adopted elements to new shot IDs without automatically starting another screening project.
