# Controlled generation and recovery

Use the available generation tools and the selected model's injected rules. Query unknown capabilities with `mstudio_models(mediaModelId)` instead of inventing parameters or switching services. For still prompts read [image prompting](../../image-production/references/prompt-writing.md).

## Reduce uncontrolled variables

Prepare only the necessary original product evidence, character and scene identity, viewpoint and states for the selected concept. Resolve reference conflicts; do not attach the whole history.

For a new visual direction, inspect one representative sample that contains the crucial relationship and the visual cast before expanding. Reuse an accepted base rather than resampling. Check clothing silhouette and colour, pose, proportions, hands, and whether the body or fabric overwhelms the product. Audience and no-face rules do not prescribe one character aesthetic.

Technical correctness is not aesthetic acceptance. If a direction is rejected, repair the sample with a meaningful change before extending its dependents; proceed under existing scope after internal checks. Use representative story moments rather than a before, during and after image for every contact. A rough layout may simplify irrelevant background, never the geometry or contact being tested. A generated overview is not a set of final frames.

## Reuse and edit by difference

For a same-view state change, edit an inspected base and keep background, product geometry, identity and light. A large viewpoint change needs original product evidence and the right scene and identity references. Reinspect the whole edit before using it as the next base; return to the last valid version if drift compounds.

A useful prompt names the output and aspect, the ordered reference roles, the stable properties, one selected state change, attention, occlusion, contact and support, compatible light, material and style, and the targeted observed defects. Under a no-face constraint specify the crop and the permitted motion, including background, reflections and screen coverage. Clean scene frames carry no labels unless requested. Avoid long generic negative lists.

Before calling, identify the minimum final frame set and generate only for new story information, a necessary state or an authorised repair. Save the actual prompt, input order, tool, exposed parameters and outputs. Do not invent seeds or promise pixel-identical repetition.

## Separate concept and execution loops

Concept changes address expectation, payoff and product relationship through [concept development](../../creative-ad-director/references/concepts.md). Generation repairs address structure, anatomy, framing and state. A failed render does not invalidate the concept; polishing material does not repair a failed premise.

| Observed failure | Response |
|---|---|
| Structure or colour drift | Return to original evidence, remove conflicting inputs, repair the part and its dependents |
| Face visible in a no-face project | Crop, reframe or remake; check action and reflections. Blur, masks and sunglasses are not exemptions |
| Disconnected limb or penetration | Clarify connection and support, simplify the contact or adjust the view while keeping the event |
| Duplicated or teleported prop | Repair the state difference and the count, not the caption |
| Local repair damages identity or background | Revert to the last valid base and narrow the change and reference set |
| Meaning exists only in captions | Revisit the concept, cues or staging |
| Motion still unknown | Keep a motion plan and validate actual footage when authorised; more stills are not motion evidence |

Default to at most two diagnosed repairs of the same defect, each with hypothesis, change and observation. That is not two mandatory calls per image or a limit on the whole task. If still stuck, stop resampling and change staging, contact or route while keeping the viewing purpose. At the user's cost or attempt limit, deliver the valid work and the explicit gaps. If no route expresses a locked event, keep it listed as not completed rather than silently replacing it.

## Perceptual realism

When the user asks for realistic, natural or less AI-looking output, judge perceived realism separately from structure, resolution and texture richness. Keep deliberately illustrated or rendered styles. Surreal events may change selected physical relationships, not every material, body or force relationship.

Before expanding a new realistic direction, inspect the sample as a whole and at key regions, and say whether it reads as a photograph or a render and which visible cues support that. Fix the baseline before extending known failures; reuse accepted samples without extra approval rounds.

Soft products deform under load, support and tension rather than behaving like rigid shells with fabric texture. Reflections, shadows and roughness follow the actual light and material. Staging serves the action instead of facing every object toward the camera in perfect symmetry. Do not add arbitrary dirt, wrinkles or noise as a realism formula or reduce product quality to imply authenticity.

Original product references constrain identity and structure; real photographic references can guide light, setting and camera; generated bases support continuity, not proof. When no real reference exists, state the unknown. Repair the identified light, staging, composition or physical cause and reinspect. Perceived realism is a visual judgement, not proof of photographic origin; an internal rating never overrides the user's concrete feedback.

## Visible product components

Before generation, list the expected visible components for the actual model: name, region, material or shape, connection and original source. Keep the list in the project, not in this Skill, and include non-featured parts.

After generation compare each expected part with the original evidence, distinguishing actual absence, justified occlusion or off-screen placement, and insufficient resolution. Do not force hidden parts into view or mark unreadable parts as verified. An expected visible part that is missing or replaced is a blocking defect even when the image looks realistic. Repair the part, recheck the whole image and the affected frames, and never propagate the faulty image as product evidence or change the view merely to evade a missing part.
