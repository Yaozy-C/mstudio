# Executing the selected shot

Convert the approved shot into visible motion and a coherent camera path. Own prompt wording, inputs and render repairs. Return conflicts in essential viewpoint, staging or revelation order to the director with evidence; do not silently replace the selected concept.

## Spatial contract

Specify the camera's starting location, viewing direction, height/distance, required visible surfaces and intended end position. Distinguish inside-looking-out from outside-looking-in. Locate openings, hinges and near edges. Light must enter through an opening or have an explicit scene source; a glowing seam alone does not justify an incompatible viewpoint. For a continuous camera move, preserve a traversable path, parallax and occlusion; do not cross closed walls. A cut must be explicit when required. No-face framing includes motion, bodies entering frame and reflections.

## Contact and motion

For each selected action, derive the necessary intermediate states from the actual starting state through the result. Identify what applies force or releases a constraint, where contact begins and ends, what supports each object, and when grip or possession changes. Carry these relationships and their visible consequences into the actual generation prompt alongside the action. A list of action names, endpoints or prohibitions does not supply this chain.

Keep identity, part count, attachment topology and actual dimensions stable. Let pose, tension, deformation, opening extent and occlusion change with force, gravity, inertia, material and mechanism travel. Resolve conflicts by preserving evidenced construction and allowing the state change the action requires. Do not invent hidden supports, springs or hardware to explain an otherwise missing cause; preserve explicitly designed departures from ordinary physics within their stated scope.

For each change, identify the enabling condition, applied force, allowed path, receiving support and resulting state. Account for changes of possession and constraints through continuous action or designed cuts. Describe only relationships needed by the selected event.

A necessary phase is one whose removal leaves a change in contact, support, constraints or possession unexplained. Keep these phases even if they are brief or visually unremarkable. Repetition and idle time may be omitted; necessary physical transitions may not be treated as redundant handling. A planned cut can skip elapsed action, with compatible poses, connections and object states on either side; a continuous take must realize its transitions without hidden cuts or morphs.

## Human performance

Describe what the person notices, wants and does in this situation. Derive gesture amplitude, speed, force, reaction latency and dialogue delivery from intention, urgency and the selected dramatic form. Choose the order and overlap of speech, gaze, breath and body movement for that interaction. Preserve plausible attention, contact and support; include behavior only when it serves the event.

## Timing and selection

Separate scene/action time, generation/source time and final-film time. The model's minimum duration applies to generation, not editorial shot length. Keep intended internal cuts explicit. An unreliable multi-event clip may need separate supported generations and shorter retained intervals; do not stretch a brief action to fill the minimum. Preserve essential passage/contact when it constitutes the proof. Transport that changes location or support remains part of the generated causal chain; only a permitted edit may omit its elapsed time. Determine the useful entry, duration and exit from the creative purpose; keep extra source handles distinct from intended film time. Retiming cannot hide a failed action.

Locate the useful entry, decisive change and exit. Sound corresponds to the visible event and material; a beat does not require a cut. Never invent final source timestamps before output exists. Actual source ranges belong to the saved shot and intended film interval; do not invent them before footage exists.

## Appearance and continuity

Keep motivated light direction, material-specific reflection, scale and contact shadows coherent throughout movement. Photographic skin and fabric require integrated lighting and natural texture, not sharpened surfaces alone. Preserve identity, clothing, product parts, scene landmarks, counts and support across occlusion and cuts. Generated references do not establish unseen product structure. Do not claim continuous motion or sound from stills.

## Product motion realism check

When a product clip drifts, floats, morphs or reads as a synthetic turntable, diagnose the motion before adding visual detail. Check product proportions and label plane, hand entry/grip/exit, base contact and weight, material response, camera speed and amplitude, and whether the light changes without a cause. Use [product motion realism](motion-realism.md) for the failure table and staging rules. Prefer one action, a larger product, a stable camera and a shorter clip over more adjectives.

## Photographic capture quality

For live-action or photorealistic work, derive color, contrast, white balance, sharpness, surface detail and optical treatment from the selected visual direction and motivated scene light. Preserve product color, identity and requested stylization. Match detail to the delivered scale. For people, prioritize facial volume, coordinated expression, bodily continuity and scene-light integration. Apply texture or capture effects only when the selected look calls for them. Repair the observed appearance problem with specific visible relationships. Use supported parameters for technical specifications; appearance cues do not guarantee output quality.

Carry the selected capture look, white balance, material finish and color relationships through the clip and across planned cuts unless the design calls for a change. Inspect whether a supplied start frame or storyboard carries an unwanted grade, plastic texture or sharpening; when it does, hand that specific appearance correction to image production rather than promising that motion prompting will remove it. An appearance-only revision preserves the selected action, timing and camera design.
