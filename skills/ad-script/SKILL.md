# Content-first screenwriting

Be responsible for what the audience actually sees, hears, expects and remembers. Deliver a piece with a clear thing worth watching, a clear point of view and a clear product relationship. "Has a hook, moves fast, resonates" are only explanations; the specific audio-visual is the work.

## Start from the current brief

Confirm what this round delivers: exploring directions, writing a full script, fixing one place, or reusing a reference film's structure with a different product. For a local revision, fix the problem directly; when the user has already chosen a direction, create inside that direction and do not reopen topic selection. When a faithful reproduction is explicitly required, study the original film's content and order of revelation, and do not turn it into an original story on your own initiative.

Extract product facts, audience and content language, communication goal, duration and presentation limits. Separate what the user has locked from what an earlier author suggested. The chat language is not the finished-film language; later revisions to the same brief keep the market, voice-over, text and duration requirements. Establish constraints separately for each brief; do not carry over the market, language or story of an adjacent case. When the finished-film language is unspecified and there is no other basis, use the current user's language for now; clarify later if the market would change the core content.

Ask only about missing information that would change the core direction. Reversible choices of scene, character or tone may proceed as creative assumptions. When evidence for a product capability is missing, narrow the claim instead of writing the whole piece as a list of prohibitions.

## Find content material

**Mstudio has no network search capability.** Use only the current project's material, user attachments, readable reference works, the existing conversation and already-imported research records. Do not call, suggest calling, or invent web/search/browser tools; do not visit the external links cited as research sources; and do not automatically delegate online research.

When you need to research the audience or reference content, look for evidence in these local materials; for how to handle it, see [Content research](references/research.md). When material is insufficient, complete a concept or draft based on known product facts with the assumptions marked, and ask only about information that genuinely affects the core direction. When the user explicitly asks for real-time trends or external verification, briefly explain that there is no network access and that you can accept material the user provides; continue with creation that does not depend on that verification. Do not make going online a precondition for delivering.

Distinguish product facts, behaviour observed in real audiences or works, the author's interpretation, and creative assumptions. "Office workers need to pack lunch" is only a scene; keep looking for specific choices, habits, trade-offs, sensory experiences, questions or relationships. A research conclusion must change some creative decision; otherwise do not pile it into the context.

Report the actions, pacing, performance and sound only of videos you have actually watched. When access is insufficient, write a provisional draft from the available evidence and state which kind of verification is missing; never call a search summary, a case-study article or a still frame a complete viewing.

## Choose the reason this piece is worth watching

Make three decisions in a short working note; do not ask the user to fill in a form:

- **Point of view**: what do you most want the audience to understand, feel, or see differently? For a short piece, favour one idea; let the other information serve it.
- **Viewing payoff**: what does the audience get - a discovery, a practical answer, recognition, a laugh, a fulfilled expectation, sensory satisfaction, an emotional or aesthetic experience? At which describable moment, or over which sustained process, does it happen?
- **The product's role**: what product action, evidence, usage relationship or recognition cue ties this payoff to the product? Category-level value may stand, but do not invent exclusive capabilities.

Do not treat dramatic conflict, reversals, characters, voice-over, a CTA or a shocking first three seconds as universal necessities. A natural process can also be watchable, but you must write the process the audience enjoys; explanatory content can also work, but it needs a question worth answering. Read [Craft and revision](references/craft.md) as needed, according to the direction.

## Develop ideas: judge first, then expand

When the creative brief is open, first explore a small number of substantially different short concepts. The difference must come from observation, point of view, viewing pleasure, the demonstration question or the product relationship; changing the title, profession and location does not count as a new idea. Do not fix a generation count to fill a form, and do not default to writing the first one.

For each concept, write a real opening, the key development and the landing point, so that it can be imagined without adjectives such as "premium, healing, viral". Test the idea first, then add detailed action. You may work backwards from the most substantial image, line or ending; keep unexpected associations, humour and stylised expression as long as they fit the brief and do not masquerade as a real product test.

When choosing, name the specific strengths and the main weakness. If the candidates share the same formula, switch to other provided material or redefine the problem; do not just swap synonyms. A weak idea is not rescued by more shots, transitions and sentimental copy. When the user wants a full script, pick the recommended direction and write it out, briefly explaining the trade-offs, without waiting for another approval; when the user wants several directions, deliver the concepts as requested and do not additionally expand them all into finished scripts.

## Write what makes it worth watching into the audio-visual

Write the action that actually happens, the exact dialogue/text and the sound that does work. Show character and relationships through specific choices, let the audience take meaning from behaviour, and avoid stuffing the author's analysis into the narration. Characters do not need to explain what is already clear on screen; equally, do not delete the explanation the user needs just to pursue a purely visual approach.

Arrange the development of information and experience: every segment should advance understanding, expectation, emotion, sensory rhythm or product evidence. Repetition can build rhythm or a running joke, but it needs variation or payoff. Setup, development, turn and resolution is not an excuse to display selling points one by one.

Preserve the viewpoint, occlusion, revelation, action rhythm, reactions and sound relationships that determine the creative. The screenwriter may think across shots but must not leave what makes the content worth watching for direction to invent. Detailed camera parameters, model prompts and task breakdowns belong to later production.

Estimate timing by the action, speech and recognition required. An explicit total length or cap must be respected; cut secondary information first, and do not silently turn 15 seconds into 23. A model's single-generation length does not automatically equal the length of an edit shot. A static script can only propose a pacing design; it cannot claim that the finished film flows.

## Do one targeted edit

Set aside your own creative notes and read the piece through as the actual audio-visual:

- What is the first thing worth paying attention to? "Putting the bag on the table" is not necessarily a problem, but why does the audience keep watching at that point?
- Is the viewing payoff still developing in the middle? Has a segment appeared only to cover a selling point?
- What is the most substantial detail? What is lost if it is removed? If long explanation is required for the piece to be watchable, rewrite the expression.
- What role does the product play in the interesting or useful part? Is the ending nothing but a tacked-on ad? Check content appeal, brand memory and purchase understanding separately.
- Would this person really say and do these things? Is there a forced misunderstanding, a fake pain point, an unsupported generalisation about the audience, or an invented product causation?

Fix the flaw that most affects this piece and keep the features that work; do not sand down its individuality just to pass every check. There is no need to score yourself, claim a hit, or self-review endlessly; deliver once the justified revisions are done. For the review method, see [Evaluation and learning](references/craft.md#evaluation-and-learning).

## Delivery

Let the user read the work first. Usually give one line of direction plus the complete audio-visual script, then explain only the key trade-offs, the basis and the important unverified items. When a direction is requested, give a concept that can be imagined; when a full script is requested, do not deliver only an outline.

Use paragraphs or tables as needed; do not generate media, add approvals or switch to another Agent by default. When saving is needed, read [Saving and handoff](references/mstudio.md) and write to the real fields under the existing permissions; chat drafts are not written into the project.

For provenance and scope, see [Research basis](references/sources.md). Its links serve provenance tracking only; visiting the network inside Mstudio is neither required nor permitted. Material supports judgement; it is not a universal formula for a hit.
