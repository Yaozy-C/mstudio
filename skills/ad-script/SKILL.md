# Content-first screenwriting

Deliver a script with one clear thing worth watching, one point of view, and a product relationship the footage can prove. "Has a hook", "moves fast" and "relatable" are explanations; the audio-visual is the work. Read [CORE.md](CORE.md) together with this file; every step below assumes its rules.

## Workflow

1. **Fix the brief.** State what this round delivers: directions to compare, a full script, one local fix, or a reference film's structure reused for another product. Extract product facts, audience and finished-film language, communication goal, duration, and voice-over or on-screen text limits. Separate what the user locked from what an earlier draft proposed. Keep this brief's market, language, duration and text rules through every revision; never inherit an adjacent brief's story. If the finished-film language is unspecified, write in the user's language and say so.
2. **Ask only what changes the core.** Scene, character and tone are reversible creative assumptions: choose and mark them. When evidence for a product capability is missing, narrow the claim rather than turning the script into a list of prohibitions.
3. **Gather material inside the project.** Read the product images, facts, existing script, references and research already in the project or the conversation. For a full rewrite, read the whole intent; for a local fix, read the target passage and its neighbours. When the audience or a reference work needs study, follow [Content research](references/research.md). Do not call, propose or simulate web, search or browser tools, and do not wait for anyone to go online; when the user asks for live trends, say that only material they provide can be used, then continue.
4. **Decide why it is worth watching.** Write a short working note with three decisions, not a form for the user:
   - Point of view: the one thing the audience should understand, feel or see differently.
   - Viewing payoff: a discovery, a practical answer, recognition, a laugh, a fulfilled expectation, sensory satisfaction or an emotional moment, and the describable moment or sustained process where it lands.
   - Product role: the action, evidence, usage relationship or recognition cue that ties the payoff to the product. Category-level value is fine; invented exclusive capabilities are not.
5. **Explore, then choose.** When the brief is open, sketch a few substantially different concepts. A real difference comes from observation, point of view, payoff, demonstration question or product relationship; a new title, job or location is not a new idea. For each concept write the actual opening, the key development and the landing so it can be imagined without adjectives. Name each candidate's strength and main weakness, pick one, and say why. A full-script request means pick and write, without another approval round; a directions request means deliver concepts, not several finished scripts. Direction-specific craft and examples are in [Craft and revision](references/craft.md).
6. **Write the audio-visual.** Write what actually happens, the exact words, and the sounds that do work. Let behaviour carry character and meaning; keep the author's analysis out of the narration, and keep the explanation the user needs. Every segment must advance understanding, expectation, emotion, sensory rhythm or product evidence; repetition needs variation or a payoff. Preserve the viewpoint, occlusion, reveal, action rhythm, reactions and sound relationships that make the piece work; do not leave that for direction to invent. Camera parameters, model prompts and task breakdowns belong to later stages. Estimate each segment by the action, speech and recognition time it needs; respect a locked total or cap by cutting secondary information first.
7. **Edit once, on purpose.** Read the draft as the finished film, not as your notes: What is the first thing worth attention, and why does the audience keep watching after it? Is the payoff still developing in the middle, or did a segment appear only to cover a selling point? What is the most substantial detail, and what is lost without it? What role does the product play in the interesting part, and is the ending more than an attached ad? Would this person really say and do this? Fix the one flaw that hurts most and keep what works; no self-scoring, no endless review. The symptom list is in [Craft and revision](references/craft.md#fix-a-flat-piece-by-locating-the-problem).
8. **Deliver.** Lead with one line of direction, then the complete script as paragraphs with title, action, on-screen text, dialogue, sound and duration, so it can be saved field for field. Then give only the key trade-offs, the basis, and what is unverified. A direction request gets a concept that can be imagined; a script request never gets just an outline. Do not generate media, add approval steps or hand off to another Agent by default. To save or hand off, follow [Saving and handoff](references/mstudio.md).

## Failure signatures

| You notice | Do instead |
|---|---|
| Every candidate is the same formula with a new job, place or title | Go back to the material or restate the problem; a difference comes from observation, payoff or product relationship |
| A segment exists only to carry a selling point | Cut it, or tie the selling point to the payoff the audience is already enjoying |
| Narration explains what the picture already shows | Delete it; keep only the explanation the user needs |
| The total runs over the cap | Cut secondary information; never announce 15 seconds and deliver 23 |
| You are about to describe a reference video you have not watched through the tools | Report only what the available access showed, and mark the rest unverified |
| The draft seems to need market validation before it can proceed | Mark the assumption and deliver the provisional draft now |
| The finished-film language drifted to the chat language | Restore the brief's language for dialogue and on-screen text |

## Report

One line of direction; the script; then trade-offs, basis and unverified items. When saving: the screenplay ID, the paragraph IDs written, the total duration against the cap, and the receipt that confirms it.

## Boundaries

Viewing order, camera, staging and continuity belong to [creative direction](../creative-ad-director/SKILL.md); image and video prompts belong to the production Skills. A finished script never authorises media generation, and a chat draft is not a saved script.
