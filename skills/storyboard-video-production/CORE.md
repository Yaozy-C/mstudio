# Core: media production

Start from the selected shot's current complete text and dialogue, using linked story context where needed. Understand its audience-facing purpose, essential change and narrative result. Ground production in the saved approved design and latest explicit user changes; a coordinator summary or old prompt does not replace them. Preserve the event's meaning when choosing inputs and organizing generation units.

Inspect relevant material to decide what it supplies, what should vary and which supported route can realize the shot within scope. Planning media need not be uploaded or determine final appearance. Explain concrete missing-input needs through the existing handoff, and carry useful observations and production reasons forward when work changes hands.

Preserve the chosen product, characters and scene direction with real references. Use concrete image asset IDs in task references; earlier submitted inputs remain historical evidence, not current truth. Do not turn an ordinary scene into glossy commercial or cinematic imagery without that direction. Preserve physical support, contact and action continuity, and use the selected model's supported parameters.

Give every referenced input a concrete purpose that names the subject and the visual use, and keep that assignment stable across segments. `role` is the technical input mode; `purpose` is the visual use.

Create video generation units of at least 5 seconds, or the selected model's higher minimum, within its supported maximum. Group adjacent, compatible short shots into one unit and one generation task. Preserve every internal shot's full description, exact duration and cut timing in the submitted prompt, including shots lasting only fractions of a second; do not impose a 1-second minimum, round them up or stretch them. Keep an explicit internal time map in existing shot text and use the unit's anchor shot ID for its task. See [generation-unit timing](references/prompt-writing.md#three-different-units) for a short final group or a route that cannot express the planned cuts.

For a selected shot-board task, use the whole four-panel board as a storyboard reference through a supported reference mode, never as the exact first/last frame. Map top-left, top-right, bottom-left and bottom-right to their actual ordered states. One continuous shot stays one full-screen uninterrupted take; panel boundaries are not cuts. Existing cut groups retain their explicit editorial map. Describe the visible change from the actual starting state through the decisive event to the ending state. Keep subject movement, camera movement and focus changes distinct; explain relevant light, material and sound changes through their causes. Stable properties remain stable. Use only factors needed by the selected shot, without adding motion or effects to satisfy a template.

An explicitly requested standalone effect or exact-control task keeps its specified format and supported input mode. Do not require a board for it merely because this Skill also handles storyboards. Apply the effect intent, target, protected region, visible progression and ending state from [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) to its actual prompt. These methods do not authorize extra paid inputs, unsupported controls or changes outside the selected effect scope.

Write the complete physical causal chain into the actual prompt, including necessary intermediate contact, support, grip and release states. Preserve structure while allowing physically required pose and deformation changes. Editorial omission requires a designed cut with compatible states; a continuous shot cannot skip a necessary transition. Remove only idle time or repetition that changes no physical state.

Report actual submission, completion and import states without implying visual approval. Detailed prompt, control and generation rules are listed in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md).
