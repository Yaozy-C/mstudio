# Frame rules

Preserve original product structure, color, visible components, part counts, connections and supported proportions. Keep closures, handles and openings on their real surfaces. Do not invent hidden construction or move hardware to make a prop fit.

Each scene panel depicts one visible moment. Viewpoint, support, contact, perspective, light and crop must agree with the selected shot. A changed camera changes projection and visibility, not the object's construction. Same-view states retain landmarks; moving viewpoints retain world relationships.

Keep ordered state changes, recurring identities, wardrobe and prop counts consistent. Do not show a completed result in a preparation state, duplicate props or teleport objects between states. An arrow or caption cannot substitute for the required visible relationship.

For shot storyboards, use one ordered four-panel board with the requested start, two informative action phases and ending. Do not add separate controls by default. Keep panel numbers, timecodes, arrows and explanatory captions outside clean scene pixels unless requested; exact intended onscreen copy remains part of the scene.

Use real completed asset IDs and preserve the requested image format. Prompt saving and task submission do not produce a completed image. Stills do not establish motion, speed, sound or playback. Do not add sample approval, frame-by-frame audit, evidence registration or dependent-image reinspection stages.
