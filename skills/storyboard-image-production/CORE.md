# Core: image execution

Storyboard boards express camera, staging and ordered scene states using the selected shared assets. Retain their art direction and finish while changing pose, framing and light for the designed scene. An asset sheet supplies identity and construction; its neutral presentation is not the storyboard format or scene lighting.

A board is four photographs sharing one setup, not four illustrations. State the photographic look once; give each panel one distinct state and one light/value decision. When the approved look is missing, an authorized full-frame look frame precedes the board. Panel scale limits skin and hand detail; do not demand what the panel cannot hold. Read [photographic direction](references/photographic-direction.md) and [skin realism](references/skin-realism.md) when appearance matters.

Preserve the selected scene, identity, outfit and original product geometry. Each scene panel shows one visible moment with a readable pose, support, contact and viewpoint; a preparation frame must not show a completed result. Use concrete surfaces and lighting instead of generic polish or quality slogans. Continuity preserves identity, construction and actual dimensions, not a frozen pose: state changes obey support, gravity, contact and material behavior. Put the relevant physical relationships into the generation prompt; do not use consistency to override them.

Execute assigned product-fidelity work and local render repairs. Use the original references to realize the director's viewpoint, lens/distance, focus and scene look. Keep construction stable while projection, occlusion, pose and visible components follow the designed view. Do not force the same product faces or source-photo angle across shots. Return a concrete design conflict to direction; repair an execution mismatch without redesigning the camera.

Use selected character, wardrobe, location and prop images as actual inputs. Their presentation does not dictate scene light or pose. Return missing shared references to the coordinator for the assets Agent.

Give every referenced image a concrete purpose — edit target, identity, product geometry, composition or state, or motion — and never rely on a generic label. Identity references do not dictate downstream pose, background or lighting. Original product photos remain structural evidence; a generated cleanup cannot prove an unseen mechanism.

For shot storyboards, deliver one temporal 2 by 2 board per requested shot, read top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right. Select four useful moments from the existing shot design, retaining necessary initial/result states and any explicit editorial cut map; do not impose four action steps or four editorial shots. A continuous shot's panels are phases of the same take, not cuts or alternate concepts. Fixed cameras retain landmarks and scale; moving cameras follow the selected traversable path. Preserve world geometry, product parts, light and counts. Keep panel explanations outside the pixels. Use the whole board as a storyboard reference handoff, never as an exact first/last frame. Explicit standalone still/control requests retain their format. Four panels are a local workflow convention, not proof of greater model compliance.

Detailed frame, reference-asset and prompt rules are listed in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md).

A shot-level storyboard regeneration replans one board under the current workflow; legacy frame counts and tasks do not mandate separate outputs. Exact unchanged task retries require an explicitly identified task or unchanged-format request. Resolve conflicting delegated output instructions before submission; preserve prior assets.
