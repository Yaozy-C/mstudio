# Object reference assets

Use for reusable props, products and their necessary construction or material views. Follow the selected project art direction and the shared [visual quality method](visual-quality.md). Assess object fidelity through structure and material behavior; human-skin rules do not describe plastic, metal, wood or cloth.

## Establish evidenced construction

Use the selected original images and supported facts to identify silhouette, proportions, visible part count, openings, closures, seams, joints, attachments and markings. A generated cleanup is an interpretation of that evidence, not proof of hidden construction. Keep unknown dimensions, mechanisms and unseen sides unknown; do not invent exact measurements or engineer a new product to complete a reference sheet.

Keep one object's construction across necessary views and states. Derive new views or local repairs from the selected source with actual inputs. A side view may expose different parts, but it must not relocate attachments, change component count or redesign the silhouette without an intended physical state change. Preserve original product markings when relevant and supported; do not invent brand text or product claims.

## Choose useful views and states

Show the surfaces needed by consuming shots with enough margin and readable scale. Use a single view, a relevant detail or a consistent view sheet as needed, without a mandatory turntable, exploded diagram or front/side/back pack. Perspective and occlusion can hide parts; do not move them to the visible face just to display everything.

Separate stable construction from state: an open lid, compressed cushion, slack strap or bent fabric retains its actual connections and follows material behavior. Show the required state only when grounded in the source or selected design. A supported surface, weight-bearing contact and plausible shadow remain necessary in an isolated asset image.

## Render the actual materials

Select the material properties that distinguish the object in this view and light. Metal depends on reflection shape and roughness; plastic on surface finish, thickness and any evidenced translucency; cloth on weave scale, seam construction, fold behavior and tension; wood on grain direction, finish and edge wear. Painted, rubber, glass or ceramic regions need their own supported response. These are conditional distinctions, not a list of effects to add to every object.

Preserve the selected color relationships and genuine differences among materials. Do not give every surface the same gloss, sharpening or random grain. Use wear, dust, fingerprints, scratches and patina only when part of the selected evidence or design; a clean object does not need artificial damage to look credible. Texture detail cannot compensate for the wrong silhouette, missing component or impossible connection.

Light the needed geometry and surfaces clearly while retaining the project's medium, stylization, material language and finish. An asset can use simpler light than the final scene without becoming a different glossy catalogue product. Avoid baking an incidental reflection, studio backdrop or staging arrangement into downstream identity requirements.

## Handoff

Return real generated image IDs, intended construction and material properties, consuming shots and unsupported or missing surfaces to the coordinator. Do not approve construction or select a candidate yourself. Execute specific revisions supplied by the user or coordinator. An image is not evidence that a real mechanism operates.
