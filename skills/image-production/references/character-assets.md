# Character reference assets

Use for identity, proportions, hair, wardrobe and assigned local repairs. Follow the selected art direction and [core](../CORE.md). A reference must make the required person or clothing usable in later shots; elaborate surface detail and an attractive background do not establish a credible identity.

## Establish one person at the needed scale

Start from selected actual images and user decisions. Preserve facial proportions, hairline, apparent age, body build and distinguishing features. Separate these from pose, expression, moisture, dirt and scene light. A name and demographic description cannot replace an actual selected identity input. A newly invented face remains a candidate.

Prioritize face volume, coordinated eyes and mouth, head/neck/body continuity and natural integration into the same lighting environment. At full-body scale, establish identity, proportions, silhouette, complete clothing and clear hand placement. Describe only surface information that can be resolved at that scale; pores, fine hairs, small skin cracks and individual fabric fibers are not default full-body requirements.

Choose a view for its consumer: face readability for identity, full body for clothing or body scale, and extra angles only for an actual need. Static, frontal, centered and formal reference poses are valid. Do not manufacture a candid moment by adding a hand-held prop, tilted head, side gaze or weight shift. When an action is required, state each hand's location, contact object and support rather than adding a generic busy-hands gesture.

## Select a usable foundation before dependent views

For a new recurring photographic character without a suitable selected image, choose an initial candidate whose face, expression and head/neck relationship can be judged. A head-and-shoulders or waist-up view often serves that need when the face would otherwise be too small; it is not a fixed lens, background or mandatory additional output. An explicitly requested full-body or single-image deliverable keeps its scope. Return a concrete identity-readability gap through coordination rather than silently adding generations or changing the deliverable.

The coordinator reviews actual candidate pixels and the user selects. If the person is not credible, address the supplied face, expression, anatomy or integration feedback before commissioning dependent outfit variants and scene boards. The generator returns candidates and factual limitations; it does not approve them or start repairs itself. Reuse a suitable selected image without a new identity-selection stage.

Derive required outfit, full-body and scene views using the selected actual identity image with an explicit subject purpose. Retain the original identity source alongside useful later views; a chain of changed generations is not proof of continuity. A new text-only portrait is a new candidate. Do not swap a face for an appealing background or combine different candidates without an explicit selected design.

Develop missing character and environment foundations separately when that helps isolate the unresolved decision. They need not be separate paid outputs when existing references suffice or a single image is requested. Preserve the user's eventual location and lighting in the consuming scene. Integration needs compatible scale, perspective, ground contact, source direction and shadows; a good isolated portrait does not prove it.

## Anatomy, wardrobe and temporary state

Match head/body proportions, limbs, neck, joints, hands and feet to the selected age and design. Keep deliberate stylization. A hand action and its contact must be legible before fine skin or textile detail. Do not invent props to occupy the hands.

Preserve required garment design, footwear, accessories, fit and colors while folds follow pose and support. Occupation or demographic labels do not authorize added scars, aging, cracked skin, dirt or a full-body wear treatment. Carry explicit work traces only at their supported locations and intensity; a dirty hand does not imply dirty clothing, face and footwear. Keep existing authored wardrobe details.

Treat sweat, oil, moisture, dirt and damage as scoped state. Damp hair does not imply a glossy face or neck. Do not add imperfections to prove realism or remove specified traces merely to make a cleaner portrait.

## Photographic appearance and local repairs

Use [photographic direction](photographic-direction.md) for camera/light and [photographic people](photographic-people.md) for a person-specific appearance question. Read [skin realism](skin-realism.md) for a visible close-up property or assigned skin repair, not as a detail bank for every person.

Describe only the actual visual decision: facial volume, coordinated expression, regional tone, light response or a resolved detail. Smooth skin, approximate symmetry, calm expressions and simple illumination can be natural. Neither retouching slogans nor mandatory pores, asymmetry, shine, grain and wear establish realism. Let scene light retain natural shadows; secondary surface texture need not be visible everywhere.

For an assigned edit, retain selected identity, age, complexion, clothing and unaffected regions while changing the diagnosed property. A waxy face may need better volume or light integration; added grit is not a general remedy. Use actual edit/reference inputs when identity must persist. Return the candidate and concrete limits without self-initiated revisions.

## Handoff

Return real image IDs, fixed identity/outfit properties, actual reference bindings, the view's purpose and unresolved requested views. The coordinator reviews and the user selects. A static image cannot establish acting, motion or sound. Do not claim that prompt changes have improved likeness or credibility without comparable actual outputs.

## Method sources and limits

[Runway's reference workflow](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/40042718905875-Creating-with-Gen-4-Image-References) demonstrates readable identity inputs and separate character/environment development. [Google's image guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation) demonstrates deriving character angles with supplied images. [Epic's Digital Humans](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/digital-humans?application_version=4.27) separates skin color, roughness, specular response, scatter and surface detail in a 3D-rendering workflow. The identity dependencies and generator-facing diagnostics above are production adaptations of these principles; they do not establish model-independent photorealism or tested output quality.
