# Character reference assets

Use for a recurring person's identity, age, anatomy, hair and wardrobe, or a local repair of those properties. Follow the selected project art direction and the shared [visual quality method](visual-quality.md). A character reference is a production source for later shots; attractive presentation alone does not establish a usable identity.

## Establish one person

Start from the selected actual images and user decisions. Separate fixed identity and outfit from temporary pose, expression, sweat, dirt and lighting. Preserve the face's proportions, hairline, apparent age, body build and relevant distinguishing features across useful views. A name and demographic description cannot substitute for the selected image. If an unseen face must be designed, identify it as a proposed design for selection, not a recovered fact.

Choose views for actual consuming shots: a readable face for close shots, complete clothing and body scale for wider shots, and additional angles only when needed. A full-body view and a face detail can be useful; they are not a mandatory sheet. Derive each view from the same selected source, retaining subject bindings. A face from one candidate and a body from another are not automatically one character.

## Select the foundation before expanding it

When creating a photographic character without a suitable selected image, establish a readable identity plate before expanding to a view sheet or many scene boards. Use [photographic direction](photographic-direction.md) and [skin realism](skin-realism.md). The plate should be head-and-shoulders or waist-up at 50–85mm, camera at eye level, with directional window light or open shade, a real room or a textured wall with depth, an unposed expression, and the selected wardrobe visible. A full-body, frontal figure on a seamless beige wall is a weak identity plate: it hides skin, hands and fabric behavior, and it pulls later scenes toward a studio catalogue look. A full-body view is justified only when a consuming shot needs body scale or the complete outfit.

If a selected identity already exists, a new text-only identity plate is a new candidate, not continuity. Bind the selected face as an actual input and change only the photographic treatment, light or background. Do not let a newly designed face silently replace the project's selected character.

Inspect the plate for synthetic tells before deriving views: waxy or uniform skin, symmetrical features, a painted hairline, plastic fabric, hidden or malformed hands, and an empty backdrop. Repair the plate rather than propagating the defect into more views. Generate the simplest useful identity candidate and return it to the coordinator for review and user selection before dependent views. Reuse an already suitable selected image without adding this stage again.

If the consuming shot genuinely needs a full-body view, stage it as a real photograph: a specific room, street or work place, directional light, one foot forward, hands occupied, the clothes in use, and the head large enough to identify. A seamless backdrop is acceptable for a pure construction or wardrobe plate, but it should not be the default character presentation.

Derive necessary angles and outfit views from that selected foundation using actual reference inputs. Keep the identity source available when using an accepted later view; do not rely only on a chain of increasingly altered generations. If the foundation has failed skin or anatomy, creating more views propagates the defect. A weak or hidden original face cannot establish an unseen frontal identity.

Prepare the character and environment separately when both need development. Combine the settled images with named identity and environment uses, preserving a separate chosen aesthetic reference where needed. Include character scale, ground contact and scene light in an assigned integration prompt; do not add a downstream test yourself. A good isolated portrait does not prove good integration.

## Anatomy and wardrobe

Match head-to-body ratio, limbs, hands, feet, neck and visible joints to the selected age and design. A child should retain child proportions rather than an adult body or coarse adult skin treatment. Preserve the user's character design in stylized work; photographic anatomy is not a reason to erase intentional stylization.

Show the required outfit, footwear and accessories at a useful scale. Keep cut, fit, fabric, seams, wear and selected colors stable while folds follow pose and support. Describe hair roots, strand grouping, silhouette and contact with skin as needed; a wet hairstyle does not imply that the entire face and neck must be glossy. Keep temporary environmental effects scoped to the requested state rather than silently baking them into every later appearance.

## Skin in photographic work

Apply this section only to a selected live-action or photorealistic look. Describe skin through the combination of anatomy, regional color, light transmission, surface response and optical detail. Texture alone cannot establish photographic credibility.

When writing the actual prompt or addressing synthetic skin, read [skin realism](skin-realism.md) and [photographic people prompts](photographic-people.md). Choose the requested photographic treatment before adding surface cues. A readable identity portrait, an environmental candid and a beauty-retouched portrait need different presentation; they are not interchangeable definitions of realism.

Describe the diagnosed properties in the actual prompt. Preserve natural, low-amplitude differences among forehead, cheeks, nose, lips, eyelids, ears and neck. Broad tonal changes should follow facial planes and the light; local warmth, translucency and fine surface relief should vary plausibly by region and person. Let surface detail resolve softly at the actual image scale. Skin may naturally be smooth, especially on a child; neither beauty smoothing nor conspicuous pores are universal realism requirements.

Do not use "flawless", "perfect", "beauty", "airbrushed", "studio softbox", "even lighting" or global "8K / HDR / ultra detailed" language for a photographic face. Those words pull toward a retouched catalogue look. Replace them with regional color, scale-appropriate pores, a light-driven sheen, fine lines appropriate to age and an unposed expression.

Distinguish dry skin, normal localized sheen and requested moisture. Where dampness is required, highlights should follow the light and moisture distribution rather than form one continuous glossy coating. Avoid inferring a wet face from damp hair or clothes. Preserve real source features and user choices; do not add freckles, acne, wrinkles, sand, sweat or grain merely as proof of realism. Fine hair, pores and small creases are conditional observed details, not a compulsory checklist to paste into every prompt.

For a user- or coordinator-assigned skin revision, address the specified light, material or tonal property while retaining identity. Do not replace a waxy surface with coarse grit, sharpen every pore or globally blur the face. Return the edited candidate for review without starting another correction yourself.

Use an actual selected photographic appearance reference when the required finish cannot be judged from words alone. Explain which visible properties it supplies; a skin-appearance reference is not permission to replace the character's face. Apply the assigned region, reflection, transmission or detail-scale change to the prompt. Rendering concepts such as subsurface scattering describe why skin looks a certain way; they are not guaranteed image-generator controls. Do not copy shader parameters, reference weights or platform-specific syntax into an endpoint that does not expose them.

## Handoff

Return real generated image IDs, intended fixed identity/outfit properties, reference bindings and any missing requested views to the coordinator. Only the coordinator reviews the output; the user selects the identity. Do not declare candidates passed, select a preferred face or initiate corrective generations. A static asset does not establish acting, motion or sound.

## Method sources and limits

[Runway's reference workflow](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/40042718905875-Creating-with-Gen-4-Image-References) demonstrates readable identity inputs and separate character/environment development. [Google's image guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation) demonstrates deriving character angles with supplied images. [Epic's Digital Humans](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/digital-humans?application_version=4.27) separates skin color, roughness, specular response, scatter and surface detail in a 3D-rendering workflow. The staged acceptance and generator-facing diagnostics above are production adaptations of these principles; they do not establish model-independent photorealism or tested output quality.
