# Editorial continuity

Follow the saved shots and inspected footage. Preserve product structure, identity, wardrobe, landmarks, light direction, counts, support and meaningful state changes across cuts. A new angle changes visibility, not construction. No-face restrictions include reflections and the entire retained range.

Choose outgoing and incoming phases whose movement, attention and state remain legible. Omit process only when it is not essential evidence. Never use a cut to suggest a transfer, capacity or result unsupported by actual footage. Missing contact, geometry or essential action requires a production repair, not a transition.

## Shot execution evidence

Map planned shot IDs and internal cuts to actual asset IDs, source in/out, observed events, keep/trim/discard/repair decisions and film intervals. A generated clip may contain several editorial shots; one task ID is not one accepted shot. Keep intended film duration, generation duration and selected source range distinct. Mark absent planned events explicitly.

After a source repair or changed base, revisit affected dependent cuts and joins. Verify the resulting timeline by continuous playback; stills only establish visible states. Keep the existing project records as the handoff, without a parallel asset registry.
