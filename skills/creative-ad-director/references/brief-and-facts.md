# Constraints, product facts and input gaps

Locate each core requirement in the user's words or the source material and label it as request, observation or hypothesis. A real feature does not establish a consumer need; a scene name does not establish motivation.

## Minimum useful record

From the conversation and existing deliverables, identify product or model, original images and specifications, use, audience task, platform and aspect ratio, language, duration, style, available production capabilities and the requested output. Do not send a questionnaire. Defaults come from the current user instructions, not from this reference. A missing video tool does not block design, but a still-image MP4 is not a continuous-action film.

Separate explicit requests, assumptions and actual acceptance. Record why a direction failed without turning a local rejection into a permanent ban on a technique. Reuse methods and confirmed settings; rebuild product facts and audience assumptions for a new product.

## From fact to demonstration

Keep a compact record per claim: fact ID, feature or claim, status and source, possible visual evidence, and the conclusions it does not support. Include prop shapes, counts and fit evidence. Do not extrapolate one region's structure to another or treat appearance as performance proof. Distinguish actual feedback from imagined personas; never invent first-hand testimonials.

A gap that affects essential structure, fit or performance blocks an evidential demonstration of it. Ask or research only as needed and choose a supported demonstration where possible; label unverified planning images. Minor visual choices are resolved through composition without blocking work.

Look for original attachments and task assets before requesting missing references. Generated images never replace original product evidence. Avoiding an unknown minor interior is acceptable; hiding whether the core function exists is not.

## Feasibility

Know the available product images, scene and character references, footage, editing and preview tools before choosing a route that depends on them. If the requested effect exceeds current capabilities, name the incomplete stage and progress on what is feasible. Keep the viewing relationship through staging, cuts or compositing rather than defaulting to a plain display because it is easier to generate.
