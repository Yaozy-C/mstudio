# Develop and repair the viewing proposition

Shared concept method for writers and directors. For a new idea, compare a few materially different viewing experiences and choose the one that fits the product, the audience and the available assets. For a revision, locate the weak relationship before changing the concept.

## State it concretely

Describe the actual opening action or line, what makes the next moment worth watching, the payoff, and the product's contribution. The opening and continuation must be concrete enough to write now; "relatable", "cinematic", "fast" and "satisfying" are not events. A familiar use case is valid; novelty and exclusive functionality are not required.

## Build the middle on the promise

Surprise can resolve and open a new question; comedy can escalate; motion or sound can develop a pattern; a demonstration can expose evidence. Keep the pauses needed to recognise a result. Another feature, another location or a later day is not automatically progression; if removing a section loses no tension, information, rhythm or product evidence, shorten or drop it.

Separate a product-enabled result from a change in the user's behaviour, and make the actual help observable without inventing a need or capability. Storytelling exaggeration cannot establish capacity, insulation or durability; use supported evidence for claims and purchase details.

## References and repair

For a reference, keep the source, the time range, the observed mechanism and the condition that makes it transferable. Reuse the relationship, not the product claims or mandatory shot lengths. A production tutorial shows how an effect was made; audience and sales results need their own evidence. Keep detailed cases in project research, not in every request.

Repair the premise when the promise or the product connection is weak; repair staging or editing when a sound idea is expressed poorly. Judge attention, continued viewing and product action separately, with comparable conditions, and revise the hypothesis from use instead of turning one outcome into a rule.
