# Naturalistic human performance

For a live-action or restrained performance style, start from the person's intention and the stimulus they actually receive, then let the response unfold in order: perception, a small recognition pause or change in gaze or breath, then a motivated bodily response. Keep human latency and overlapping motion without imposing the same pause on every event or slowing urgent action.

## What to specify

- Eyes may shift, blink and refocus; breathing can subtly move chest and shoulders. Speech, gaze, head, breath and weight need not start and stop together. No mechanical open-mouth, speak, close-mouth cycle.
- Choose a few context-driven secondary actions: releasing finger tension, adjusting a garment, shifting support, swallowing, a brief look away. Do not schedule every micro-gesture or fill stillness with random motion.
- Express emotion as a progression, not a stock facial endpoint: surprise may interrupt a hand action before the eyes lift and the head turns; sadness may begin with silence, a shallower breath and a lowered gaze; pleasure may show in the eyes before a slight smile. These are examples, not prescriptions. Keep the brief's dramatic intensity, comic exaggeration or deliberate stillness when chosen.
- In an interaction, establish who hears or sees what and when. A listener reacts to the cue available, not to an unheard line. Avoid synchronised turns, gestures and reactions across people; respect written overlap or interruption. Keep weight, inertia, asymmetry and small imperfections without changing identity or adding story events.

## Example

If consistent with the script: a person folding a garment hears their name, briefly stops the fold, lifts their eyes toward the speaker, then turns their head; the held garment settles after the torso. This describes an observable causal order, fitted to the actual duration and the reference starting pose; it is not a template for every reaction.

## Locks, prompts and checks

Separate identity and wardrobe locks from performance freedom. Keep the chosen scene light and skin appearance; do not import a white studio background into a location shot. Do not add pore inventories, universal beauty constraints, fixed lenses or long negative lists to motion prompts. Keep required speech and interaction timing explicit while avoiding lockstep mouth, head and body movement.

Static frames establish pose and gaze; only playback shows reaction latency, unintended synchronisation, breathing and lip sync. Report observed defects only; do not infer acting quality from prompt length or texture detail.
