# Animation principles as visible performance

Use for action design, motion review, or a pose/contact problem that needs more than the prompt guide. Choose principles that materially improve the shot's purpose, material behavior and style. Do not append a twelve-principle slogan or require all twelve in every shot.

| Principle | Apply through visible decisions |
|---|---|
| Squash and stretch | Specify load, deformation, timing and recovery to convey weight or elasticity. Rigid realistic products must not stretch arbitrarily; soft objects respond to support and material. Exaggeration must preserve recognizable identity and structure. |
| Anticipation | Prepare a decisive action through weight shift, reverse motion, loading or attention. A surprise can intentionally omit preparation; an omitted cause is different. Avoid a long wind-up for every action. |
| Staging | Make the main action legible through view, silhouette, pose, occlusion and sequencing. Contact and result must read; secondary detail must not compete. See [shot grammar](cinematography.md). |
| Straight ahead and pose to pose | Design necessary key poses for precise causality/contact; allow evolving behavior for fluids or smoke. Specify start, decisive poses and result without requiring an image for every pose or pretending an unverified model offers frame-level control. |
| Follow through and overlapping action | Distinguish when the body and attached parts respond. Describe lag and settling; avoid simultaneous stops or detached floating attachments. |
| Slow in and slow out | Choose speed curves for initiation, effort, impact and recovery. Impacts may stop abruptly and mechanisms may move uniformly. Universal smooth easing makes action soft. |
| Arcs | Follow joints, pivots and inertia where appropriate. Keep straight constrained mechanisms straight. Arcs cannot justify penetration or changing pivots without cause. |
| Secondary action | Support the main action, emotion or speed through subordinate gestures and environmental responses. Specify priority and order, not simultaneous noise. |
| Timing | Allocate time by mass, distance, force and reading needs. Separate completion, recognition and reaction. Equal duration divisions or model limits must not erase required beats. |
| Exaggeration | Identify the pose, speed contrast or reaction being amplified. Preserve other relationships; do not fabricate product performance as proof. |
| Solid drawing | Maintain perspective, weight, support, volume and occlusion through poses. Rich textures cannot compensate for weightlessness, penetration or missing parts. |
| Appeal | Use readable silhouettes, pose rhythm, contrast and character. Appeal does not mean cute, symmetrical or polished. Under no-face constraints, express performance through body, hands, objects and timing. |

## Turn principles into beats

Use the phases the shot needs: initial state/motive, effort or start, decisive contact/change, result, and recognition/reaction. Phases can merge or be deliberately omitted, but preserve the causality, joke or value evidence that depends on them. Record what the camera sees at each relevant point and when it follows, stops or cuts. Subject speed and camera speed are separate.

For selected principles, specify the affected object, trigger, path/pose/speed change, sequence relative to the main action and observable acceptance point. Weight must arise from material-specific response, not a universal bounce recipe.

Reaching the endpoint and delivering a performance are separate outcomes. A gag that requires a recognition pause still fails without it. No-face work still needs performance. Inspect staging, poses and attention statically; inspect timing, speed, weight and reaction dynamically. Extracted stills alone cannot establish rhythm.

## Inputs and diagnosis

Motion references constrain action and timing; appearance references constrain identity and material. They must be compatible. If a rough proxy obscures the interaction, add necessary spatial/contact cues. If a detailed proxy is copied as appearance, remove irrelevant styling or change the route. Previs requires actual tools and assets; a plan is not an executed result.

Diagnose missing action source, misplaced attention, incorrect key pose, wrong speed curve, missing reaction or conflicting reference appearance. Repair the corresponding design/input instead of appending natural, lively or powerful, or globally speeding up the clip. Retain current retry, budget and authorization limits.

The classic framework is Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston's The Illusion of Life.
