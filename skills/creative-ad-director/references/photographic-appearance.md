# Photographic people and environmental light


Apply these methods when the selected brief calls for photographic people or live-action scenes. Preserve the requested art direction, location, ethnicity, age, body type and skin tone. Realism does not imply a fixed palette, lens, geography or beauty standard. Describe the visible cause of an appearance rather than stacking cinematic, 8K or dermatological-quality claims; resolution still belongs in supported parameters.

For people, prioritize facial volume, coordinated expression, head/neck/body continuity and integration into the same photographic environment. Match surface detail to framing; pores, vellus hair, lines, localized shine and wear belong only when the selected person, view or assigned repair needs them. Smooth skin, neutral poses and approximate symmetry can be correct. Preserve intentional makeup, age features and styling. Natural shadow and optical falloff may hide secondary texture; do not add fill or sharpening simply to display every material. A catchlight must agree with its actual source.

Specify motivated sources, direction, softness, exposure relationships and relevant room or weather cues. The following are optional setups to adapt, not mandatory defaults:

- Interior day: openings and present practical sources establish the light; retain their direction and the surfaces that reflect or absorb it. Do not assume every room has soft window light or pale wood.
- Interior night: practical lamps can motivate low illumination, with weak cooler window ambience where plausible. Keep shadow separation without making night look like day. Waiting, companionship or loneliness comes from the selected story, not the time of day alone.
- Overcast exterior: broad skylight can coexist with strong material colors, local contrast and reflected light. Humidity or haze requires an actual weather or design reason.
- Soft sunny exterior: softened or bounced daylight with a clear direction, controlled highlights and readable shadows; maintain actual weather and time. Do not erase a deliberately hard-sun design.

Choose surface colors and their area relationships with the composition before choosing a grade. Photographic work can be vivid or restrained, crisp or diffuse, high- or low-contrast. Low saturation, warm/cool separation, halation, grain and diffusion are available only when they serve the selected look; none is a default proof of photographic quality. Grain must not stand in for skin texture, and diffusion must not hide required detail. A 35mm or 50mm perspective is a choice, not a rule. Choose framing and depth of field so required environment and spatial relationships remain legible. For a new look, use [photographic exploration](photographic-exploration.md).

Carry the selected palette, source relationships and degree of diffusion consistently across related shots, adapting to justified location/time changes. Use targeted corrections for visible plastic skin, excessive sharpening or lost background information instead of appending the entire negative list to every request.
