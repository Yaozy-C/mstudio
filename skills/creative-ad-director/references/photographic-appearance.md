# Photographic people and environmental light


Apply these methods when the selected brief calls for photographic people or live-action scenes. Preserve the requested art direction, location, ethnicity, age, body type and skin tone. Realism does not imply a fixed palette, lens, geography or beauty standard. Describe the visible cause of an appearance rather than stacking cinematic, 8K or dermatological-quality claims; resolution still belongs in supported parameters.

For skin visible at the selected scale, retain irregular texture, fine lines and restrained local color variation. Close views can include subtle vellus hair and micro-shadowing, with localized oil sheen on raised facial areas rather than a plastic or waxy surface. Match skin detail to focus and distance. Preserve intentional makeup, age marks and styling. Sharpness should support natural optical detail without halos, etched pores or repeated noise patterns. A catchlight must agree with the light source.

Specify motivated sources, direction, softness, exposure relationships and relevant room or weather cues. The following are optional setups to adapt, not mandatory defaults:

- Interior day: window light as a principal source, with a warmer practical lamp only if present in the scene; preserve facial modeling and readable shadow detail. Off-white, pale wood, gray-blue and light brown are one possible restrained palette.
- Interior night: practical lamps can motivate low illumination, with weak cooler window ambience where plausible. Keep shadow separation without making night look like day. Waiting, companionship or loneliness comes from the selected story, not the time of day alone.
- Overcast exterior: broad soft skylight, gentle contrast, optional humidity or haze supported by weather. Gray-blue, gray-green, dry green and light brown can support a restrained look while skin remains plausible.
- Soft sunny exterior: softened or bounced daylight with a clear direction, controlled highlights and readable shadows; maintain actual weather and time. Do not erase a deliberately hard-sun design.

For an explicitly chosen restrained realist look, consider low-to-moderate saturation and contrast, slight warm/cool separation, gentle highlight diffusion (subtle black mist / black pro-mist), restrained halation and fine organic film grain. Apply only the effects that serve the reference; grain must not stand in for skin texture, and diffusion must not hide required detail. A 35mm or 50mm perspective is a choice, not a rule. Choose framing and depth of field so required environment and spatial relationships remain legible.

Carry the selected palette, source relationships and degree of diffusion consistently across related shots, adapting to justified location/time changes. Use targeted corrections for visible plastic skin, excessive sharpening or lost background information instead of appending the entire negative list to every request.
