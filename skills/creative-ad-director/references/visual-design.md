# Visual design: make choices before adding adjectives

Use the selected concept, product evidence and user's style constraints. Do not turn every image into the same dark luxury advertisement.

## Start with a visible proposition

Find the relationship that rewards looking: a gesture in its setting, an encounter of shapes or colors, a scale contrast, material behavior or a discovery within the chosen event. Decide how attention moves through the picture. One dominant subject can organize it; several interacting details can also sustain it. Do not impose an absolute focal hierarchy on every photograph. For open photographic exploration, use [the photographic method](photographic-exploration.md) before fixing the look. A product floating in particles is not a proposition unless the relationship means something in this concept. An effect can reveal construction, contrast states or connect spaces; it need not appear at all.

Translate emotional language into a hypothesis about visible relationships. "Cold and severe" might mean restrained expression, hard lateral separation and controlled negative space, not simply a blue cast. It might instead require a different solution for a warm-coloured brand. Mark the hypothesis as a design choice, not proof that a model understands an emotion.

## Make a compact look decision

Record only decisions that change this shot:

| Decision | Specify | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | A focal priority or the relationships among several points of interest; what rewards further looking | Assuming either clarity or complexity guarantees an interesting image |
| Composition | Viewpoint, subject scale, crop, silhouette gaps, negative space | "Beautiful composition" |
| Value and colour | Hue, value, chroma and area relationships in actual surfaces and light; separation or deliberate blending | Treating a global grade or a fixed palette as the composition |
| Light | Source direction, apparent size, falloff and reflected shape | "Cinematic lighting" alone |
| Material | Which reflections, transmission, roughness or folds reveal the material | Applying the same shine to skin, metal and cloth |
| Motion | What new information camera/subject movement reveals | Orbit, push, zoom and shake without a reason |
| Restraint | Elements to exclude because they compete with the proposition | Decorative particles on every product |

Do not manufacture focal length, exposure or physically implausible light settings merely to look technical. Use numerical values only when relevant and supported; perceptible geometry and light relationships are still required.

## Transfer reference properties, not entire pictures

For each real reference, identify the subject it supplies, properties to borrow, properties not to borrow, and conflicts with other references. Product evidence wins for construction; identity evidence wins for identity. A style reference may supply highlight shape without supplying its product or background. A motion reference may supply direction and timing without supplying a new face.

Do not pretend to have inspected unavailable references. When no reference is supplied, label a written look as proposed rather than reference-matched. Do not invent image asset IDs.

## Separate three different deliverables

An action board explains ordered events. A look frame establishes appearance at full frame. A control frame is an actual supplied state for an endpoint that supports that control. One artifact can serve two purposes only when its content and the endpoint genuinely permit it.

Preserve an explicitly selected four-panel board workflow. Explain when an independent keyframe would help; do not silently substitute it, crop a board or generate additional images. A beauty complaint alone is not permission to multiply calls. Conversely, an explicit standalone hero frame must not become a four-panel board just because the image agent also makes storyboards.

## Explore selectively, then keep the selected direction

When the authorized budget includes exploration, compare a small number of distinct hypotheses at the same scope and cost. Change a purposeful design variable: a hard graphic shadow versus a broad reflection, for example. Do not call three random rerolls three visual directions.

Prefer pairwise reasoning anchored in the brief: which candidate keeps the product legible, realizes the chosen lighting relationship and leaves the intended space? Keep ties and uncertainty. There is no universal numerical beauty threshold. A new reference or user's preference can legitimately change the selection.

Once selected, retain the look across the scene group. Repair execution locally rather than reopening the concept. Stop when the authorized comparison/repair budget is exhausted; return the best available option and remaining defects.

## Design an effect as an event

Before production, answer in ordinary handoff text:

- Why does the effect belong in this shot, and what should be revealed at its end?
- What changes, what must stay recognizable, and what must be preserved exactly?
- What triggers the change, where is its moving boundary, and how does it settle?
- What passes in front of or behind what, and why does the camera move or stay still?
- What real input states and available route are needed, and what would count as failure?

Keep the handoff compact. These are planning questions, not required JSON fields. Add detail for the hard relationship, not a mandatory worksheet for every simple shot.

A preset description may inspire a mechanism. Do not assume it exposes the preset's hidden control pipeline or that its name is a callable capability. A full-object transformation and an unchanged silhouette conflict; resolve whether the effect acts on the surroundings, a surface layer, a limited region or the whole object. Do not silently choose on the user's behalf.

## Handoff through existing roles

Provide the selected look, borrowed/excluded reference properties, important states, effect scope, motivated motion and failures to watch for in existing permitted text or the ordinary response to the coordinator. Do not add database fields or claim extra permissions. When more images are needed, the existing coordinator can arrange work according to its permissions; the director need not invent direct agent calls.

Production must compile the relevant decisions into actual request text and verified reference bindings. An elegant handoff that never enters the model input does not control generation. Return unresolved design constraints instead of burying them under longer prose.

## Example: separating metal and matte surfaces

For a device whose curved metal edge and matte body should be readable, choose a close three-quarter composition with a silhouette gap between the device and its case. A narrow side-rear source makes a shaped edge reflection; restrained front fill keeps the matte face readable. Keep the brightest background region away from the brightest edge. Leave one clean area for the planned graphic; do not generate a speculative logo there.

A brief calling for flat graphic lighting or an everyday setting needs a different lighting arrangement.
